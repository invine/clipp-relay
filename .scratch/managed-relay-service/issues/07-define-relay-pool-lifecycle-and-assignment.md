# Define single Relay Instance lifecycle and discovery

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 02, 06

## Question

How should one single-process Relay Instance generate and publish its ephemeral identity and public addresses, expose readiness and capacity, answer Relay Discovery, drain during a non-overlapping rollout, recover after restart, and prevent stale exact Rendezvous records from remaining reachable?

## Comments

- Q112 settled: every process start generates a fresh Ed25519 libp2p key and WebRTC certificate from the operating-system CSPRNG and keeps both only in memory; neither is stored in PostgreSQL, a Kubernetes Secret, or a volume.
- Q113 settled: production WSS hostname and public ports come from Helm configuration; namespace-scoped read-only Kubernetes watches obtain TCP/UDP public addresses from the named Services; optional explicit overrides support preallocated IPs and development; the Relay Instance contributes its Peer ID and WebRTC certhash.
- Q114 settled: discovery publishes one atomic Relay Discovery Document only after every enabled transport has a bound listener and enough public information for a complete multiaddr. Administratively disabled transports are omitted rather than blocking publication.
- Q115 settled: Kubernetes readiness represents usable local process, listeners, and publication state plus absence of draining/stopping. PostgreSQL failure and capacity saturation affect admission responses rather than data-plane readiness, while liveness never depends on PostgreSQL.
- Q116 settled: discovery returns `429 rate_limited` with a retry hint at hard process capacity and `503 temporarily_unavailable` while required policy dependencies are unavailable or the instance is draining/stopping. Existing Relay Sessions and circuits continue within valid local state and credit; successful discovery adds no capacity data.
- Q117 settled: Relay Drain immediately withdraws discovery, becomes Kubernetes-unready, rejects new Relay Authentication, HOP reservations/connects, and Rendezvous registrations, allows existing circuits a bounded completion period, then closes all remaining Relay Sessions and exits.
- Q118 settled: v1 uses a one-replica StatefulSet and stable `relay-0` Relay Network Slot with ordered replacement. The old Pod drains and fully terminates before its replacement starts, accepting a short planned outage rather than exposing two ephemeral identities behind one slot.
- Q119 settled: Relay Sessions, reservations, Rendezvous Leases, Peer ID, and WebRTC certificate never survive restart. The new process begins empty and publishes only its new identity; stale documents fail dial or Peer-ID verification and trigger rediscovery, requiring no database tombstone, epoch, or stale-record cleanup job.
- Q120 settled: when an observed Kubernetes Service public address changes, the Relay Instance atomically publishes a new Relay Discovery Document with the same process-lifetime Peer ID and WebRTC certhash. If any enabled transport loses the information needed for its public multiaddr, the complete document is withdrawn and the instance becomes unready until the full enabled transport set is publishable again.
- Q121 settled: the Relay Instance may retain its last complete public-address snapshot only for a configurable staleness window while Kubernetes list/watch refresh is unavailable. A document's `validUntil` is capped by that window; expiry withdraws discovery and readiness until a successful refresh.
- Q122 settled: Kubernetes readiness does not require public self-dials. It verifies local listeners, configured routing inputs, observed Service status, atomic publication, and lifecycle state; external end-to-end probes monitor Internet reachability without controlling Pod readiness.
- Q123 settled: the unauthenticated operational HTTP surface is limited to `/livez`, `/readyz`, and Prometheus-compatible `/metrics`. The Kubernetes startup probe uses `/readyz`; v1 exposes no separate startup or detailed diagnostic endpoint.
- Q124 settled: only exhaustion of the configured service-wide active Relay Session capacity makes Relay Discovery return `429 rate_limited`. Unauthenticated handshake bounds, libp2p Resource Manager limits, reservation and circuit Safety Limits, and Relay Account quota are enforced at their respective operations without globally withdrawing discovery.
- Q125 settled: Relay Drain is an idempotent lifecycle transition triggered by `SIGTERM`, with `SIGINT` equivalent for local operation. V1 exposes no public or administrative drain endpoint.
- Q126 settled: while draining, existing Relay Sessions may carry already-admitted Relayed Traffic and use Ping plus Rendezvous Lookup and Unregister. Relay Authentication, reservation creation or renewal, HOP Connect, and Rendezvous Register are rejected.
- Q127 settled: drain completion waits for active circuits, not idle Relay Sessions, reservations, or Rendezvous Leases. When the active circuit count reaches zero, all remaining sessions close and the process exits; the configurable deadline force-closes remaining circuits, and Kubernetes `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` exceeds that deadline by a shutdown margin.
- Q128 settled: the explicit process lifecycle is `Starting`, `Serving`, `Draining`, then `Stopped`. Recoverable loss of public-address publication leaves the live process in `Serving` but makes discovery and readiness unavailable; database and capacity failures affect admission rather than lifecycle state.
- Q129 settled: each successful discovery response calculates `validUntil` as the earlier of the configured discovery TTL and the end of the current public-address snapshot's staleness window. Exact duration defaults are deferred to the initial-defaults ticket.
- Q130 settled: discovery is advisory and does not reserve capacity. Relay Authentication atomically acquires a Relay Session slot; discovery changes immediately between `200` and `429` as capacity becomes available or exhausted, without a queue or hysteresis in v1.
- Q131 settled: same-account reauthentication on an existing connection needs no additional slot. A new connection for the same Peer ID may replace an existing Relay Session at full capacity as an atomic one-for-one swap after every authentication and quota check succeeds; failure leaves the previous session intact.
- Q132 settled: `/metrics` exposes only aggregate, low-cardinality lifecycle, discovery-availability, connection, pending-handshake, Relay Session capacity, reservation, active-circuit, Resource Manager denial, authentication-outcome, drain-result, and charged-byte measurements. Relay Account IDs, Peer IDs, credentials, email addresses, and public addresses never appear as metric labels.
- Q133 settled: an in-memory atomic active-circuit count driven by stock relay tracer open/close events controls drain completion. It resets on process start and retains no circuit identities or Relay Account association, preserving the decision not to build a circuit registry.
- Q134 settled: the first termination signal begins Relay Drain; a second signal or the drain deadline force-closes circuits and sessions. `/livez`, `/readyz`, and `/metrics` remain available until final HTTP shutdown, with `/readyz` returning `503` throughout the drain.
- Q135 settled: `/livez` and `/readyz` return only stable plain-text `200 ok` or `503 unavailable` responses. Detailed availability reasons appear in aggregate metrics and logs rather than public health responses.
- Q136 settled: the Relay Discovery Document includes every valid observed public address, with deterministic internal ordering and deduplication. An explicit per-transport override replaces Kubernetes discovery for that transport and removes that transport's dependency on Kubernetes Service status.

## Answer

Each Relay Instance is exactly one process lifetime. On every start it creates a fresh Ed25519 libp2p key and WebRTC certificate from the operating-system CSPRNG and retains them only in memory. Its Peer ID, certhash, Relay Sessions, reservations, Rendezvous Leases, and circuit count are never restored after restart. A new process therefore starts empty and publishes only its new identity. A client holding an obsolete document will either fail to dial or fail Peer-ID verification and rediscover; the service needs no identity epoch, tombstone, or stale-record cleanup job.

The process obtains production WSS routing information and public ports from Helm configuration and watches the named Kubernetes Services with namespace-scoped read-only permissions for public TCP and UDP addresses. Explicit per-transport overrides replace Kubernetes discovery for that transport and support preallocated addresses and development. Every valid observed address is included, deduplicated, and deterministically ordered internally. The process publishes a Relay Discovery Document atomically only when every administratively enabled transport has a bound listener and a complete public multiaddr containing the current Peer ID and, where required, certhash. Disabled transports are omitted. A change republishes the entire snapshot; loss of any required transport withdraws it until the complete enabled set is available again.

A Kubernetes list/watch interruption may reuse the last complete Public Address Snapshot for a configurable staleness window. Every successful discovery response computes `validUntil` as the earlier of `now + discovery TTL` and the end of that snapshot's staleness window. Expiry withdraws discovery and readiness until Kubernetes state is refreshed. Readiness relies on local listeners, configured routing inputs, observed Service status, a publishable atomic snapshot, and lifecycle state; it does not perform public self-dials. External probes own end-to-end Internet reachability monitoring.

The explicit lifecycle is `Starting`, `Serving`, `Draining`, then `Stopped`. Loss of publication is recoverable within `Serving`: liveness remains healthy while readiness and discovery are unavailable. PostgreSQL failure and capacity saturation affect admission rather than lifecycle or data-plane readiness. The unauthenticated operational surface is limited to `/livez`, `/readyz`, and Prometheus-compatible `/metrics`; the startup probe uses `/readyz`. Health responses expose only stable plain-text `200 ok` or `503 unavailable` results. Metrics and stdout logs carry detailed reasons.

Relay Discovery returns `503 temporarily_unavailable` when required policy dependencies are unavailable, the complete address snapshot cannot be published, or the process is draining or stopping. It returns `429 rate_limited` with a retry hint only when the configured service-wide active Relay Session capacity is exhausted. Discovery does not reserve a slot and successful discovery exposes no capacity fields. Relay Authentication remains authoritative and atomically acquires a slot; availability changes immediately as the count changes, with no queue or hysteresis. Same-account reauthentication on its existing connection needs no new slot. After every authentication and quota check succeeds, a new connection for the same Peer ID may atomically exchange an existing Relay Session for one new session even at full capacity; failure leaves the old session intact. Handshake bounds, Resource Manager limits, reservation and circuit Safety Limits, and per-account Relay Quota remain operation-specific controls and do not globally withdraw discovery.

The aggregate `/metrics` surface covers lifecycle and discovery availability, connections and pending handshakes, current and maximum Relay Sessions, reservations, active circuits, Resource Manager denials, authentication outcomes, drain results, and aggregate charged bytes. Labels remain low-cardinality and never contain Relay Account IDs, Peer IDs, credentials, emails, or public addresses. Drain completion uses only an in-memory atomic active-circuit count driven by stock relay tracer open and close events; it retains no circuit identities or Relay Account association and resets on start.

V1 occupies one stable `relay-0` Relay Network Slot through a one-replica StatefulSet with ordered, non-overlapping replacement. The old Pod drains and terminates completely before its replacement starts, intentionally accepting a short outage instead of routing one slot to two ephemeral identities. The first `SIGTERM`, or `SIGINT` during local operation, idempotently enters Relay Drain: discovery is withdrawn, readiness returns `503`, and new Relay Authentication, reservation creation or renewal, HOP Connect, and Rendezvous Register are rejected. Existing circuits continue, while authenticated sessions may still use Ping and Rendezvous Lookup or Unregister.

The drain waits only for the active circuit count. Once it reaches zero, every remaining Relay Session closes and the process shuts down. A second termination signal or the configurable deadline force-closes circuits and sessions. Health and metrics endpoints remain available until final HTTP shutdown, and Kubernetes `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` exceeds the drain deadline by a shutdown margin. Exact time and capacity defaults are selected by the dedicated initial-defaults ticket.
