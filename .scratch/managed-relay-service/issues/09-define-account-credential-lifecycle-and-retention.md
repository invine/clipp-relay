# Define account credential, lifecycle, and retention policy

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 03

## Question

What exact Relay Account identity, pending/active/suspended/denied transitions, Google and administrator authorization, access-token and rotating renewable-grant lifetimes, replay handling, account-wide revocation, self-deletion boundary, metadata retention, and privacy-limited audit behavior should the initial service guarantee without creating a device registry?

## Comments

- Q137 settled: one canonical Google External Identity maps to exactly one Relay Account. `(issuer, sub)` is unique and stable behind a separate random internal account ID, so an email change continues resolving to the same account.
- Q138 settled: any successfully authenticated Google External Identity may create its own Pending Relay Account. Administrators do not pre-create accounts by email, and repeated or concurrent first logins resolve atomically to the same account.
- Q139 settled: v1 account states are `Pending`, `Active`, `Suspended`, and `Denied`. Pending accounts have portal access but no Relay credentials; Active accounts may obtain and use them; Suspended accounts are temporarily blocked without losing administrative assignments or usage history; Denied identities are refused and cannot recreate another account. Self-deletion remains a separate lifecycle decision.
- Q140 settled: v1 has one administrator role derived entirely from the Secret-backed Google email allowlist. Every administrator request re-evaluates the current allowlist and Google-authoritative email metadata; Relay Account status does not affect administrator authority, and there are no database-managed roles or permissions.
- Q141 settled: each successful Google login refreshes the last verified email, `email_verified`, and `hd` presentation metadata without changing Relay Account identity or status. Ordinary accounts may use non-Gmail Google identities; the stricter Google-authoritative email rule applies only to administrators.
- Q142 settled: browser Portal Sessions and Clipp runtime credentials are disjoint. Each successful registered-public-client authorization creates a Login Grant bound to client type but not a device and issues Access Tokens with fixed Relay Discovery and Relay Authentication permissions; Relay tokens carry no administrator authority.
- Q143 settled: first Google login creates `Pending`; an administrator may move `Pending` to `Active` only while assigning a Quota Plan, or to `Denied`; may move `Active` to `Suspended` or `Denied`; may move `Suspended` to `Active` or `Denied`; and may return `Denied` to `Pending` for review before a separate approval. No account state changes automatically.
- Q144 settled: entering `Suspended` or `Denied` atomically revokes every Login Grant and Relay Access Token, then invalidates all live Relay Sessions for that Relay Account within ten seconds. Reactivation never restores credentials, so each Clipp runtime must be authorized again.
- Q145 settled: every Relay Account state may establish a Portal Session through Google to inspect its status and use permitted privacy or revocation actions. Only an Active Relay Account may complete Clipp client authorization or use relay services.
- Q146 settled: authorization transactions live for 10 minutes, one-use authorization codes for 5 minutes, Relay Access Tokens for 15 minutes, and Portal Sessions have a 30-minute inactivity limit plus 12-hour absolute lifetime. They have no expiry grace, and v1 configuration may shorten but not extend these security ceilings.
- Q147 settled: a Login Grant has a 30-day inactivity limit and fixed 180-day absolute lifetime. Successful rotation resets inactivity without extending the absolute deadline.
- Q148 settled: refresh tokens are strictly single-use with no retry or overlap window. Reuse of any consumed generation revokes the Login Grant and all derived Relay Access Tokens; loss of a response after committed rotation may require a new browser authorization.
- Q149 settled: Clipp credentials remain independent after successful Google login. The service retains no Google tokens and performs no periodic Google checks, so Google outage, logout, email change, or account disablement does not itself revoke Login Grants; bounded lifetimes and explicit user or administrator revocation are authoritative in v1.
- Q151 settled: a configurable service-wide Safety Limit permits at most 20 active Login Grants per Relay Account. Further client authorization is rejected until grants are revoked; the service never silently evicts an older grant.
- Q153 settled: an account owner may use `Sign out everywhere` and an administrator may use `Revoke credentials` to revoke every Login Grant and Relay Access Token and close every live Relay Session for the Relay Account without changing its status, Quota Plan, overrides, or usage history.
- Q154 settled: self-deletion and every administrator mutation, including account status, Quota Plan, and override changes, require a Google authentication no more than ten minutes old. Viewing information and revoking credentials require only a valid Portal Session.
- Q155 settled: a Portal Session is an opaque server-side record referenced by a rotated `__Host-` prefixed `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax` cookie. Every state-changing POST requires an allowed-origin check and synchronizer CSRF token; credentials and selectable redirect targets never appear in cookies or URLs.
- Q150 settled: v1 neither lists nor exposes actions for individual Login Grants. The portal may show only an aggregate active-grant count and offers only account-wide `Sign out everywhere`, avoiding device-like presentation or implied device identification.
- Q152 settled: no user or administrator operation revokes an individual Login Grant, so Relay Sessions never need an ephemeral Login Grant association. The strict refresh-reuse response from Q148 may still invalidate the one compromised Login Grant automatically; deliberate credential revocation uses the account-wide behavior from Q153.
- Q156 originally settled: `Sign out everywhere` is available from the account portal and every authenticated Clipp runtime. The server commits account-wide revocation before the initiating client reports success and erases its local credentials. The in-app operation and completion promise is superseded by Q212 in [Define Clipp runtime integration](10-define-clipp-runtime-integration.md); the server-side commit-before-success rule remains unchanged.
- Q157 settled: `Sign out everywhere` also invalidates every Portal Session for the Relay Account. The initiating portal returns confirmation while clearing its own cookie, after which a new browser or client authorization requires Google authentication.
- Q158 settled: owners of Pending, Active, Suspended, and Denied Relay Accounts may request irreversible self-deletion after recent Google authentication. Deletion first performs account-wide revocation and terminates every Relay Session and Portal Session; administrators suspend or deny accounts rather than deleting another person's account.
- Q159 settled: consumed refresh-token hashes remain only while their Login Grant is active for reuse detection. Authorization transactions, codes, Relay Access Tokens, Portal Sessions, and ended Login Grants are purged within 24 hours after expiry, redemption, or revocation; raw credential values are never retained, and security audit events follow their separate policy.
- Q160 settled: account-linked quota history retains the current Weekly Quota Window plus the previous 12 completed windows. Older account-linked usage is deleted while anonymous aggregate service metrics may remain.

Audited events are account creation, account status and quota changes, account-wide revocation, credential issuance failure, refresh reuse, and self-deletion. Peer IDs, IP addresses, user agents, tokens, Google claims, request bodies, free-form notes, and routine successful discovery, Relay Authentication, refresh, or traffic activity are excluded.
- Q162 settled: self-deletion permits the next Google login to create a fresh Pending Relay Account while a Retained Quota Usage record follows the External Identity through the current Weekly Quota Window. It retains only a keyed External Identity digest, window identifier, and Quota Committed; re-registration transfers that consumption to the new account, whose newly assigned Quota Plan defines the allowance. Concurrent sessions reset, and prior status, plan, overrides, credentials, email metadata, and older usage do not return. The retained record is deleted when the window ends.
- Q163 settled: a Pending Relay Account is automatically deleted after 90 days without a successful Google portal login. This automatic cleanup creates no deletion record, so a later login may create a new Pending Relay Account.
- Q164 settled: Active, Suspended, and Denied Relay Accounts do not expire for inactivity. They remain until owner self-deletion or an administrator state transition, preserving suspension and denial decisions without silently restoring relay access.
- Q165 settled: an existing Relay Account retains the canonical Google issuer and `sub`, only the latest verified email, `email_verified`, `hd`, and last-validation timestamp. It stores no email history, name, avatar, contacts, or Google tokens.
- Q166 settled: current account status is checked before authorization-code issuance, code exchange, Login Grant refresh, Relay Discovery, and Relay Authentication introspection. A credential flow begun while Active fails if the account becomes blocked before completion; portal login remains available in every state.
- Q167 settled: an allowlisted Administrator Identity may approve its own Relay Account. The Secret-backed allowlist is the root of trust and the action follows the same audit requirements as every other administrator mutation; v1 has no second-administrator approval workflow.
- Q168 settled: retained audit events have administrator-only read access, append-only application writes, and no edit or delete API. Relay Account owners cannot see administrator identities; cryptographic audit ledgers and protection from direct database operators are out of scope.
- Q169 settled: stdout operational logs contain only ephemeral request and Relay Session correlation IDs plus structured outcomes. Stable Relay Account IDs, External Identities, emails, Peer IDs, credentials, and public addresses are excluded; durable identity-bearing accountability exists only in the restricted audit trail.
- Q170 settled: self-deletion revokes access immediately and transactionally creates the Retained Quota Usage and audit event. Within 24 hours it deletes the account row, raw External Identity, email metadata, status, Quota Plan, overrides, account-linked quota history, authorization records, and Portal Sessions; only the temporary Retained Quota Usage and de-identified audit events survive.
- Q171 settled: self-deleting a Denied Relay Account clears the previous denial. The identity may immediately create a fresh Pending Relay Account, still requiring a new administrator approval, while current-window consumption follows through the Retained Quota Usage.
- Q172 settled: audit events retain the deleted account's old random internal ID for their 180-day lifetime, but every mapping from that ID to an External Identity or email is deleted and a newly registered account is never linked to those events.
- Q173 settled: account creation, status and quota changes, Account-wide Revocation, self-deletion, and refresh-reuse revocation commit their audit event in the same PostgreSQL transaction as the mutation. If the audit write fails, the mutation fails closed.
- Q174 settled: an authenticated account owner sees the exact account state and a generic state-specific explanation, but not internal reason codes, Administrator Identities, or audit history. OAuth, Relay Discovery, and Relay Authentication failures remain non-specific.
- Q175 settled: authorization codes, Relay Access Tokens, refresh-token generations, Portal Session identifiers, state, and nonce are independent 32-byte CSPRNG values encoded as unpadded base64url. PostgreSQL stores only HMAC-SHA-256 digests using a versioned Secret-backed pepper; raw values exist only while received or issued and never enter logs.
- Q176 settled: refreshing a Login Grant does not revoke its previously issued Relay Access Tokens. They remain valid until their 15-minute expiry unless refresh-reuse response, Account-wide Revocation, or an account state change invalidates them.
- Q177 settled: a durable credential-failure audit event is created only when the failure is attributable to a resolved Relay Account and registered public client and is security-significant: blocked status, Login Grant limit rejection, or refresh reuse. Malformed requests, unknown tokens, invalid codes, and Internet scanning produce only aggregate metrics and redacted operational logs.
- Q178 settled: automatic expiry of an abandoned Pending Relay Account records a `pending_expired` audit event with its old random account ID, timestamp, and successful outcome while retaining no External Identity or email mapping.
- Q179 settled: refresh-token reuse invalidates its Login Grant and derived Relay Access Tokens, but already authenticated Relay Sessions remain until their fixed expiry because they retain only a Relay Account association. Other Login Grants remain valid; Account-wide Revocation is required for immediate termination.
- Q180 settled: credential-digest configuration supplies one current HMAC pepper plus any retiring peppers in a Kubernetes Secret. New records use the current version and validation tries every configured version. A retiring pepper remains until all protected credentials and Retained Quota Usage records expire and pass their 24-hour cleanup deadline; early removal safely invalidates affected credentials without a raw or unkeyed fallback.
- Q181 settled: credential issuance and account mutation serialize on the Relay Account's PostgreSQL row. Issuance either commits first and is invalidated by the following mutation, or the mutation commits first and issuance fails; no credential survives a committed blocked or deleted state.
- Q182 settled: suspension, denial, reactivation, quota changes, and administrator credential revocation require one structured reason from `routine_administration`, `policy_enforcement`, `suspected_abuse`, `security_response`, or `support_correction`. Audit records contain no free-form note.
- Q183 settled: ordinary portal `Log out` revokes only the current Portal Session and does not affect Clipp relay credentials. `Sign out everywhere` remains the distinct Account-wide Revocation action and ends all Portal Sessions.
- Q184 settled: the 24-hour deletion guarantee applies to online application records, not existing encrypted PostgreSQL backups. Backups age out under the future backup-retention contract and are never queried by the running service; restoration must apply deletion and retention rules before serving, with the exact anti-resurrection mechanism deferred to the backup-and-restore decision.
- Q185 settled: Portal reads and mutations, Google callbacks, code exchange, refresh, discovery authorization, and new Relay Authentication fail temporarily and closed during PostgreSQL unavailability. No account or credential operation uses cached or in-memory fallback state; already established Relay Sessions follow their separately settled local credit and expiry bounds.
- Q186 settled: administrator mutations use optimistic account revisions. A mutation based on a stale revision returns a conflict and requires reloading rather than overwriting another administrator's change.

## Answer

One canonical Google External Identity, identified by issuer and `sub`, maps to exactly one Relay Account with a separate random internal account ID. Email never identifies the account. Any successfully authenticated Google identity may create its own Pending Relay Account, while repeated or concurrent first logins resolve atomically to the same account. Each successful Google login replaces the stored presentation metadata with only the latest verified email, `email_verified`, `hd`, and validation timestamp. The service stores no Google tokens, email history, name, avatar, or contacts. Existing Clipp credentials do not depend on later Google availability, logout, email change, or account disablement.

The account states are `Pending`, `Active`, `Suspended`, and `Denied`. First login creates Pending. An administrator may approve Pending as Active only while assigning a Quota Plan, deny Pending, suspend or deny Active, reactivate or deny Suspended, and return Denied to Pending for a separate review and approval. No transition occurs automatically. Pending, Suspended, and Denied owners may still authenticate to the portal and see their exact state with a generic explanation, but only Active accounts may authorize Clipp clients or use the relay. Internal reason codes, Administrator Identities, and audit records are not exposed to account owners or protocol clients.

V1 has one administrator role. It is derived entirely from the current Secret-backed Google email allowlist and requires the authoritative-email checks established by the Google authorization research. Every administrator request re-evaluates the allowlist; Relay Account status is irrelevant to administrator authority. An allowlisted administrator may approve its own Relay Account, which supports bootstrap and is audited normally. Administrator mutations require a Google authentication no more than ten minutes old and use optimistic account revisions so a stale update fails with a conflict. Suspension, denial, reactivation, quota changes, and administrator credential revocation require one structured reason: `routine_administration`, `policy_enforcement`, `suspected_abuse`, `security_response`, or `support_correction`. V1 has no database roles, multiple administrator roles, second-person approval, or free-form administrator notes.

Portal Sessions and Clipp runtime credentials are separate. A Portal Session is an opaque server-side record referenced by a rotated `__Host-` prefixed `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax` cookie. State-changing requests require both an allowed-origin check and synchronizer CSRF token. A Portal Session has a 30-minute inactivity limit and 12-hour absolute lifetime. Ordinary portal `Log out` ends only the current Portal Session and does not alter Clipp credentials.

Each successful registered-public-client authorization creates one Login Grant bound to the public client type, never to a Device Identity, physical device, installation identifier, Peer ID, IP address, or user-agent fingerprint. The authorization transaction lasts 10 minutes and its one-use authorization code lasts 5 minutes. A Login Grant has a 30-day inactivity limit and fixed 180-day absolute lifetime. Refreshing resets inactivity but never extends the absolute deadline. A service-wide Safety Limit permits 20 active Login Grants per Relay Account; further authorization is rejected until Account-wide Revocation or natural expiry, without silently evicting an older grant.

Relay Access Tokens are opaque bearer credentials with fixed Relay Discovery and Relay Authentication permissions and no portal or administrator authority. They expire after 15 minutes. Refreshing a Login Grant rotates its strictly single-use refresh-token generation with no overlap or retry window, but previously issued Access Tokens remain valid until their own expiry. Reuse of any consumed refresh generation revokes the Login Grant and all Access Tokens derived from it. A response lost after a committed rotation may therefore require new browser authorization rather than weakening replay detection.

V1 neither lists nor exposes an action for an individual Login Grant. The portal may show only an aggregate active-grant count. Relay Sessions retain only their Relay Account association, not a Login Grant association, so reuse-triggered revocation of one Login Grant prevents new use but leaves already authenticated Relay Sessions alive until their fixed expiry, bounded by the remaining 15-minute Access Token lifetime. Other Login Grants remain valid.

The deliberate revocation action is Account-wide Revocation: `Sign out everywhere` for an owner and `Revoke credentials` for an administrator. Revocation is performed and confirmed in the portal; Clipp's account-management handoff is defined by Q212 in [Define Clipp runtime integration](10-define-clipp-runtime-integration.md), replacing the earlier in-app operation and completion promise. The server first commits revocation of every Login Grant, Relay Access Token, and Portal Session, then closes every live Relay Session for the account within ten seconds; the portal reports success only after commit. Account status, Quota Plan, overrides, and usage history remain unchanged. Entering Suspended or Denied performs the same credential invalidation and live-session termination. Reactivation never restores credentials.

Self-deletion is available to Pending, Active, Suspended, and Denied owners after Google authentication no more than ten minutes old. Administrators suspend or deny accounts rather than deleting another person's account. Deletion immediately performs Account-wide Revocation and transactionally creates its audit event and any required Retained Quota Usage record. Accepted Q232 in [Define internal Go modules and PostgreSQL persistence](14-define-go-modules-and-postgresql-persistence.md) further requires raw identity/presentation detachment in that transaction, immediately freeing identity uniqueness for re-registration. The hidden old account and its remaining status, plan, overrides, account-linked quota history, authorization records, and Portal Sessions are physically purged in bounded work within 24 hours. The private deletion marker is not a fifth public account state.

Self-deletion permits the same External Identity to register a fresh Pending Relay Account immediately, including after deleting a Denied account. It does not reset current-week usage. A Retained Quota Usage record retains only an HMAC-protected digest of the External Identity, the current Weekly Quota Window identifier, and Quota Committed. Re-registration during that window transfers the consumption to the new account; its newly assigned Quota Plan determines the allowance. Concurrent sessions reset because deletion closes them. Previous status, plan, overrides, credentials, email, and older usage do not return. The retained record is deleted when the window ends.

Abandoned Pending Relay Accounts are automatically deleted after 90 days without a successful Google portal login and may register again later without a Retained Quota Usage record. Active, Suspended, and Denied accounts never expire for inactivity. Account-linked quota history retains the current Weekly Quota Window and the previous 12 completed windows; older linked usage is deleted while anonymous aggregate metrics may remain. The retained consumption total is Quota Committed under accepted Q221 in [Define internal Go modules and PostgreSQL persistence](14-define-go-modules-and-postgresql-persistence.md), not exact durable transferred-byte statistics. That ticket's accepted Q225 clarification names the deleted-account anti-reset record Retained Quota Usage; the retention behavior is unchanged.

Consumed refresh-token hashes remain only while their Login Grant is active for reuse detection. Authorization transactions, authorization codes, Relay Access Tokens, Portal Sessions, and ended Login Grants are purged within 24 hours after redemption, expiry, or revocation. All authorization codes, Access Tokens, refresh generations, session identifiers, state, and nonce values are independent 32-byte operating-system CSPRNG outputs encoded as unpadded base64url. PostgreSQL stores only HMAC-SHA-256 digests using versioned Secret-backed peppers. New records use the current pepper; validation also tries configured retiring peppers until every protected record and Retained Quota Usage has expired and passed cleanup. Removing a pepper early invalidates affected credentials without any raw or unkeyed fallback. Accepted Q241 in [Define internal Go modules and PostgreSQL persistence](14-define-go-modules-and-postgresql-persistence.md) additionally rejects key removal while it protects unexpired Retained Quota Usage; starting with that key missing blocks new-account registration until restoration or the affected window ends, rather than resetting usage.

Accepted Q239 in that persistence ticket keeps recoverable outer Clipp login state only in a bounded process-local continuation, with an authoritative digest-only database transaction and a separate opaque browser-binding cookie. Both continuation and transaction are required; restarting the relay cancels unfinished Google sign-ins, not otherwise-valid Portal Sessions or Login Grants. Accepted Q240 derives a stable per-session CSRF token from the Portal Session credential and server pepper, stores only its digest, and retains the required Origin checks. These rules add no raw credential/state storage in PostgreSQL.

Security and administrative audit events are retained for 180 days and are administrator-readable, append-only through the application, and unavailable through edit or delete APIs. Each contains only timestamp, structured event type, outcome and reason code, an optional target account's random internal ID, an optional internal Quota Plan ID, the normalized allowlisted email for an acting administrator, and public client type when relevant. Account creation, state and quota changes, plan creation/archival, Account-wide Revocation, security-significant credential issuance failure, refresh reuse, self-deletion, and `pending_expired` are audited. The optional plan target and plan event types are the accepted Q238 extension in [Define internal Go modules and PostgreSQL persistence](14-define-go-modules-and-postgresql-persistence.md). Mutating events and their audit events commit in the same PostgreSQL transaction or fail together. After deletion, audit rows may retain the old random account ID but no mapping to an External Identity, email, Retained Quota Usage, or newly registered account.

Credential failures create durable audit events only when attributable to a resolved Relay Account and registered public client and caused by blocked status, the Login Grant limit, or refresh reuse. Malformed requests, unknown tokens, invalid codes, Internet scanning, and routine successful discovery, Relay Authentication, refresh, or traffic produce only aggregate metrics and redacted operational logs. Stdout logs use ephemeral request and Relay Session correlation IDs and structured outcomes; they exclude stable Relay Account IDs, External Identities, emails, Peer IDs, credentials, and public addresses.

All credential issuance and account mutations serialize on the Relay Account's PostgreSQL row. An issuance racing suspension, revocation, or deletion either commits first and is then revoked, or observes the committed mutation and fails; no credential survives a blocked or deleted state. Accepted Q234 in [Define internal Go modules and PostgreSQL persistence](14-define-go-modules-and-postgresql-persistence.md) adds an account credential-invalidation generation, separate from its administrator revision, which also fences older account-bound authorization flows and codes; ordinary plan/override changes do not advance it. Accepted Q239 handles initially unknown accounts using temporary process-local External Identity revocation fences, without account IDs or Device Identities, checked when Google resolves the identity; older flows cannot complete after revocation or deletion/re-registration. Portal operations, Google callbacks, code exchange, refresh, discovery authorization, and new Relay Authentication fail temporarily and closed when PostgreSQL is unavailable. Only already established Relay Sessions continue under their separately settled local-credit and expiry rules.

The 24-hour deletion guarantee covers online application records, not pre-existing encrypted PostgreSQL backups. Backups remain unavailable to the running service and age out under the future backup-retention contract. A restore must apply deletion and retention rules before serving traffic; the exact anti-resurrection mechanism remains part of the outstanding backup-and-restore decision.

Accepted Q275 in [Define the Helm and Argo CD deployment contract](11-define-helm-and-argo-cd-deployment-contract.md) separately permits restricted operator-only ingress/database diagnostics containing IPs, database identifiers or error details for at most seven days, including exported copies. This is an infrastructure exception, not a change to the strict application stdout/audit rules above. Request-query/body, credential/header and SQL statement/parameter logging remain disabled; discovered credential leakage is a defect. Infrastructure diagnostics are not an application account-history store or a substitute for the privacy-limited audit schema.
